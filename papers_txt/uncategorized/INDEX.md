## Cell-autonomous adaptation: an overlooked avenue of adaptation in human evolution
**File:** `Cell-autonomous_adaptation__an_overlooked_avenue.txt`
**Subjects:** N/A
**URL:** https://www.cell.com/trends/genetics/fulltext/S0168-9525(24)00260-9

This perspective introduces cell-autonomous adaptations (CAAs) — inherited traits that enhance individual cell function and thereby boost organismal fitness. Unlike adaptations requiring multicellular organization (e.g., organ-level changes), CAAs can manifest in isolated cells. Examples include adaptations to hypoxia and cold in high-altitude and Arctic populations. The authors show that CAAs parallel adaptations in unicellular organisms, and by analyzing selection signals in Andean highlanders, they identify novel candidate CAAs. The concept bridges unicellular and multicellular evolution, suggesting that many important adaptations in complex organisms originate at the cellular level and can be studied experimentally in simpler systems.

## Following evolutionary paths to protein-protein interactions with high affinity and selectivity
**File:** `Following_evolutionary_paths_to_protein-protein.txt`
**Subjects:** N/A
**URL:** https://www.nature.com/articles/nsmb.1670

This study traces the evolutionary paths by which new protein–protein interactions with high affinity and selectivity emerge, using the colicin–immunity protein (ColE–Im) system as a model. Starting from Im9, which weakly inhibits ColE7, the authors apply iterative rounds of mutagenesis and selection — first for affinity to ColE7, then for selectivity against ColE9 — achieving ~10⁵-fold affinity increase and 10⁸-fold selectivity increase. Structural and functional analysis of intermediates reveals that interface evolution proceeds through "generalist" intermediates and exploits latent, promiscuous binding configurations. Compensatory stabilizing mutations play a key role, paralleling enzyme evolution strategies.

## Scalable continuous evolution for the generation of diverse enzyme variants encompassing promiscuous activities
**File:** `Scalable_continuous_evolution_for_the.txt`
**Subjects:** N/A
**URL:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-020-19539-6?utm_source=chatgpt.com

This paper uses OrthoRep — a continuous directed evolution system in yeast — to evolve *Thermotoga maritima* tryptophan synthase β-subunit (TmTrpB) across many independent replicates, selecting only for primary tryptophan synthesis activity. By running many parallel evolution experiments at depth (>100 generations each), the authors generate sequence-diverse TmTrpB variants with expanded promiscuous substrate profiles useful for industrial biocatalysis (synthesis of tryptophan analogs). The study demonstrates that evolutionary depth and scale, rather than targeted screening for promiscuous activities, can naturally yield functionally diverse variant collections — mimicking how ortholog diversity arises in nature.

## Continuous germinal center invasion contributes to the diversity of the immune response
**File:** `Continuous_germinal_center_invasion_contributes.txt`
**Subjects:** N/A
**URL:** https://www.cell.com/cell/fulltext/S0092-8674(22)01507-0

This paper demonstrates that naive B cells continuously invade established germinal centers (GCs) well beyond the initial seeding period. Using multi-color fate-mapping and single-cell BCR sequencing, the authors show that late-arriving B cell clones can successfully compete with incumbent clones, contributing to clonal diversity within GCs. This ongoing influx refreshes the GC repertoire and enables adaptation to evolving targets. The findings challenge the classical view that GCs are seeded during a narrow window and then evolve in isolation, and have implications for vaccine strategies and understanding chronic infection responses.

## Extrachromosomal circular DNA: biogenesis, structure, functions and diseases
**File:** `Extrachromosomal_circular_DNA__biogenesis,_structure,.txt`
**Subjects:** N/A
**URL:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s41392-022-01176-8?utm_source=chatgpt.com

This comprehensive review covers extrachromosomal circular DNA (eccDNA) — small circular DNA molecules found outside chromosomes. It discusses the biogenesis pathways (homologous recombination, microhomology-mediated repair, replication errors), structural features, and methods for detection and characterization. The paper reviews eccDNA functions including gene amplification (particularly in cancer, where ecDNA drives oncogene overexpression and drug resistance), roles in aging, immune responses (via cGAS-STING pathway activation), and potential as liquid biopsy biomarkers. The review covers eccDNA across organisms from yeast to humans and discusses therapeutic implications.

## CRISPR-C: circularization of genes and chromosome by CRISPR in human cells
**File:** `CRISPR-C__circularization_of_genes_and.txt`
**Subjects:** N/A
**URL:** https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/46/22/e131/5078801?utm_source=chatgpt.com

This paper introduces CRISPR-C, a CRISPR/Cas9-based method for generating extrachromosomal circular DNA (eccDNA) in human cells. By making two CRISPR cuts flanking a genomic region, the intervening linear fragment circularizes via non-homologous end joining (NHEJ), producing defined eccDNA molecules. The authors demonstrate circularization of regions ranging from individual genes to large chromosomal segments. The tool enables controlled study of eccDNA biology, including replication, transcription from circular templates, and gene amplification mechanisms relevant to cancer. CRISPR-C provides a programmable system for investigating eccDNA function and dynamics in mammalian cells.

## Complete set of ORF clones of Escherichia coli ASKA library (A Complete Set of E. coli K-12 ORF Archive): Unique Resources for Biological Research
**File:** `Complete_set_of_ORF_clones.txt`
**Subjects:** N/A
**URL:** https://academic.oup.com/dnaresearch/article/12/5/291/350187?login=false

This paper describes the construction of the ASKA library — a complete set of individually cloned open reading frames (ORFs) from *E. coli* K-12. Each ORF is His-tagged at the N-terminus and GFP-fused at the C-terminus, cloned into a plasmid under IPTG-inducible control with tight repression by LacIq. SfiI restriction sites enable easy transfer to other vectors. The library supports systematic functional genomics including protein production and purification, localization studies via GFP fluorescence, protein–protein interaction analysis, and DNA microarray construction. Two versions exist: with and without GFP fusion, providing flexible resources for large-scale *E. coli* biology.
