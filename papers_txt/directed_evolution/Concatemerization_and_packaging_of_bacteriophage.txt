DISCUSSION 
The procedure 
of T7 DNA

and 
packaging 
used here is at least an order of magnitude 
more 
efficient 
in producing 
infectious 
particles 
than 
previous

used 
(Sadowski, 
1977; 
Kuem- 
merle and Masker, 
1977; Serwer 
et al., 1983). 
Effi- 
ciencies 
this high are necessary 
to determine 
cause- 
effect relationships 
between 
events. 
For example, 
the 
observation 
in Fig. 2 that DNA packaging 
is associated 
with 
both shortening 
of concatemers 
and increase 
in 
the intensity 
of integral-length 
concatemer 
bands sug- 
gests 
that the DNA packaged 
was 
cut from the con- 
catemers. 
Previous 
observations 
suggest 
that in vivo j 
concatemers 
are at least 
partially 
packaged 
before 
being cut to mature size (Serwer 
and Watson, 
1981). If 
the DNA packaging 
efficiency 
had been 1 O-fold lower, 
the effect 
of packaging 
on concatemers 
would 
not 
have been visible. 
It appears 
that the improvement 
in 
efficiency 
of the procedure 
used here is primarily 
the 
result of combining 
in extracts 
both Dextran 
10 and all 
T7 gene products. 
In a previous 
study 
(Serwer 
ef al., 
1983), 
the use of Dextran 
10 was 
introduced 
to in- 
crease 
DNA packaging 
efficiency; 
the use of a similar 
polymer 
was 
found necessary 
for the in vitro packag- 
ing of T3 DNA using a procedure 
with purified compo- 
nents (Hamada 
et a/., 1986). However, 
in the study of 
Serwer 
et al. (1983) the products 
of T7 genes 3 (endo- 
nuclease), 
5 (DNA 
polymerase), 
and 6 (exonuclease) 
(Studier and Dunn, 
1983) were 
not in extracts. 
Using 
procedures 
otherwise 
identical to those 
used here, it 
has not been possible 
to achieve 
DNA packaging 
effi- 
ciencies 
of greater than 0.14/o in the absence 
of these 
three gene products 
(data not shown). 
Efficiencies 
ob- 
tained with the procedure 
of Hamada 
et al. (1986) are 
also 
lower 
than 
those 
achieved 
here. 
Apparently, 
some gene products 
are not needed to achieve some 
DNA packaging, 
but at least one of these 
gene prod- 
ucts is needed 
to achieve 
efficiencies 
comparable 
to 
those 
reported 
here. 
The speed of concatemerization
