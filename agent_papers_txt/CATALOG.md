## Bacterial lysozyme inhibitors — active-site occlusion (2 papers)

_Added 2026-09-17. **The load-bearing pair for HEWL-based experimental design.** Gram-negative bacteria carry periplasmic/outer-membrane-anchored lysozyme inhibitors in four families — **Ivy**, **PliC/MliC**, **PliI** (I-type), **PliG** (G-type) — and the structurally characterised ones inhibit by **inserting a loop into the lysozyme catalytic cleft**, contacting HEWL's catalytic residues **Glu35 and Asp52** directly. ## hGH as model antigen — REJECTED alternative (5 papers)

_Added 2026-09-18. Supporting the **rejected** human-growth-hormone option in [Grant_application/INSERM_IRP_2026/MODEL_ANTIGEN.md](../Grant_application/INSERM_IRP_2026/MODEL_ANTIGEN.md). Two strands: (a) the **evolution** case — GH really does evolve in episodic bursts (Wallis 1996: near-stasis at ~0.2–0.3 × 10⁻⁹ subs/aa/yr, interrupted by bursts at 25–50× that rate in artiodactyls and primates, plus further bursts in amphibia, elasmobranchs and teleosts; Liu 2001 dates the primate burst to the simian common ancestor); and (b) the **display** case — monovalent pIII phage display was *invented* on hGH and hGH was affinity-matured on the immobilised receptor ECD (Bass 1990 → Lowman 1991 → Lowman & Wells 1993, ending at ~400-fold). **Two caveats that matter more than the headline claims:** (1) ⚠️ Liu 2001 finds **dN/dS < 1 everywhere** — cite it for *episodic rate acceleration*, never for "positive selection on GH" — and explicitly reports that GH and GHR changed at **only 1 of 13 shared intermolecular contacts**, so residue-for-residue ligand–receptor co-evolution is **not** supported.
